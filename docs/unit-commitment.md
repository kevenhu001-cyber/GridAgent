# Single-area unit commitment and storage

The existing --math-program / --math-resume interface accepts family unit_commitment. This is a deterministic physical-model compiler, not a second optimizer. Input fixes hourly demand, thermal capacity and minimum output, linear marginal energy cost, hourly online cost, startup/shutdown costs, normal ramps and startup/shutdown power limits, minimum up/down hours, initial power and duration in initial state. Storage has separate power/energy ratings, fixed initial and terminal SOC, charge/discharge efficiencies and optional discharge cost. Every interval is exactly one hour. Currency is a three-letter code.

The compiler creates continuous power and binary online/startup/shutdown variables per unit/hour and charge/discharge/SOC/binary mode variables per battery/hour. Transition equations, exclusive switching, minimum output, capacity, ramp and rolling minimum state constraints preserve chronology. Initial residual minimum-state time is enforced. A transition near the final hour may create an obligation carried beyond the horizon; the report records the residual duration, not an assertion that future demand is feasible. Storage charge and discharge are mutually exclusive. The SOC equation is E[t] = E[t-1] + eta_charge*P_charge - P_discharge/eta_discharge. Generation plus net discharge equals demand each hour; no load shedding is silently introduced. Objective is total currency over the horizon.

Source canonicalization sorts equipment IDs. The reservation binds source and compiled mathematical program digests and compiler version. The existing SciPy HiGHS MILP engine handles the generated program. In addition to generic matrix verification, the physical verifier independently reconstructs chronology, generation limits, transition/ramp feasibility, initial and ongoing dwell time, storage capacity and energy, mutually exclusive charge/discharge, demand balance, and total costs from source fields and returned values. Reports include schedule, residuals, and remaining terminal obligations. Solver optimality remains solver-reported, not independently proven. A feasible mathematical solution must pass both verifiers.

Bounds: at most 24 hours, 12 units and 6 batteries individually, subject to the stricter existing 200-variable and 500-row combined budget. Actual feasible combinations may be smaller. Positive finite equipment bounds and efficiency floors protect numerical conditioning; not a general large-scale UC solver. No network constraints, reserve constraints, renewable forecast model, reactive power, AC feasibility, security contingencies, degradation, stochastic scenarios or actual equipment control. Initial conditions and terminal obligations must be used explicitly for consecutive scheduling windows. This does not replace the existing multi-bus PyPSA dispatch family.

Tests include analytically checkable 65-CNY commitment and 90-CNY storage examples, carryover, infeasible chronology and ramp cases, invalid inputs, deterministic compilation, independent verification and durable resume. CI must validate implementation.
